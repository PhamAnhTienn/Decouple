\subsection{Subspace-Specific Reliability Estimation}
\label{sec:reliability}
 
SARM does not assign a single reliability value to an entire modality.
Instead, it estimates separate reliability signals for its shared and
modality-specific representations.
The distinction lies in the evidence used to support each subspace.
 
 
\paragraph{Shared-space structuring.}
Shared reliability is assessed from cross-modal corroboration.
For this comparison to be meaningful, shared representations from
different modalities should follow a compatible coarse semantic
organization.
We therefore use cross-modal prototype alignment (CPA) as an auxiliary
structuring objective.
 
Let
$C=[c_1,\ldots,c_{K_p}]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{K_p\times d}$
denote $K_p$ normalized prototypes shared across modalities, and let
%
\begin{equation}
    Z^{c,m}
    =
    [z_{m,1}^{c},\ldots,z_{m,B}^{c}]^\top,
    \qquad
    A^m=Z^{c,m}C^\top
    \label{eq:prototype_similarity}
\end{equation}
%
denote the shared representations and their prototype affinities for a
mini-batch of $B$ samples.
 
Following balanced prototype assignment
\citep{caron2020unsupervised,kim2023swamp}, we obtain:
%
\begin{equation}
    Q^{m,*}
    =
    \arg\max_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}
    \left[
        \operatorname{Tr}(Q^\top A^m)
        +
        \epsilon H(Q)
    \right],
    \label{eq:prototype_assignment}
\end{equation}
%
where
$H(Q)=-\sum_{i,k}Q_{ik}\log Q_{ik}$ and
$\mathcal{Q}$ imposes balanced sample--prototype marginals,
$Q\mathbf{1}_{K_p}=\mathbf{1}_B$ and
$Q^\top\mathbf{1}_B=\frac{B}{K_p}\mathbf{1}_{K_p}$.
The assignment is solved using Sinkhorn--Knopp normalization
\citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn}.
 
Prototype prediction for modality $m$ is defined as:
%
\begin{equation}
    p_{ik}^{m}
    =
    \operatorname{softmax}_{k}
    \left(
        \frac{(z_{m,i}^{c})^\top c_k}{\tau_p}
    \right),
    \label{eq:prototype_prediction}
\end{equation}
%
and assignments from one modality supervise prototype predictions in
another.
The resulting alignment objective is:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CPA}}
    =
    -\frac{1}{M(M-1)B}
    \sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}
    \sum_{\substack{n\in\mathcal{M}\\n\neq m}}
    \sum_{i=1}^{B}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K_p}
    \operatorname{sg}
    \left(
        Q_{ik}^{m,*}
    \right)
    \log p_{ik}^{n}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cpa}
\end{equation}
 
This objective provides a coarse common organization for shared
representations without requiring pointwise alignment.
The resulting shared space is then used for sample-level reliability
estimation.
 
 
\paragraph{Shared reliability.}
Since the shared subspace is intended to capture common information,
its reliability can be assessed through within-sample cross-modal
corroboration:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    u_{m,i}^{c}
    =
    1
    -
    \frac{1}{M-1}
    \sum_{\substack{
        n\in\mathcal{M}\\
        n\neq m
    }}
    \cos
    \left(
        z_{m,i}^{c},
        z_{n,i}^{c}
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shared_uncertainty}
\end{equation}
%
A larger $u_{m,i}^{c}$ indicates weaker support from the corresponding
shared evidence in the other modalities.
 
 
\paragraph{Complement-conditioned specific reliability.}
The same criterion is unsuitable for modality-specific
representations, since cross-modal differences may encode useful
complementary information rather than unreliability.
We therefore assess specific reliability from same-modality local
support under a comparable complementary shared context.
For modality $m$, the complementary shared context is:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \widetilde{z}_{m,i}^{c}
    =
    \operatorname{Norm}
    \left(
        \sum_{\substack{
            n\in\mathcal{M}\\
            n\neq m
        }}
        z_{n,i}^{c}
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:complementary_context}
\end{equation}
 
This estimator follows a conditional locality assumption:
samples with similar complementary shared semantics are expected to
exhibit locally coherent patterns within the same modality-specific
subspace.
Importantly, this does not require specific representations from
different modalities to be aligned.
 
Let $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ denote the training index set.
For each modality, we define a reference memory:
%
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{B}^{m}
    =
    \left\{
        \left(
            \widetilde{z}_{m,j}^{c},
            z_{m,j}^{s},
            j
        \right)
        :
        j\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:reference_memory}
\end{equation}
 
The $K_n$ most comparable references are selected according to their
complementary shared contexts:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{N}_{K_n}^{m}(i)
    =
    \operatorname{TopK}_{
        j\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}\setminus\{i\}
    }
    \cos
    \left(
        \widetilde{z}_{m,i}^{c},
        \widetilde{z}_{m,j}^{c}
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:semantic_neighbors}
\end{equation}
 
Specific uncertainty is then evaluated from the corresponding
same-modality representations:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    u_{m,i}^{s}
    =
    1
    -
    \frac{1}{K_n}
    \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{K_n}^{m}(i)}
    \cos
    \left(
        z_{m,i}^{s},
        z_{m,j}^{s}
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:specific_uncertainty}
\end{equation}
%
Thus, $u_{m,i}^{s}$ measures the lack of local support rather than
cross-modal disagreement.
The retrieved representations serve only as references for reliability
estimation and are not injected into the current representation.
 
 
\paragraph{Uncertainty-to-reliability mapping.}
The two uncertainty measures are converted into reliability signals
using the same monotonic mapping:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    r_{m,i}^{c}
    &=
    \operatorname{sg}
    \left[
        \max
        \left\{
            r_{\min},
            e^{-u_{m,i}^{c}/\tau}
        \right\}
    \right],\\
    r_{m,i}^{s}
    &=
    \operatorname{sg}
    \left[
        \max
        \left\{
            r_{\min},
            e^{-u_{m,i}^{s}/\tau}
        \right\}
    \right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reliability_mapping}
\end{equation}
%
Here, $\tau>0$ controls the decay rate and
$0<r_{\min}<1$ prevents complete suppression.
The resulting reliability scores are detached from gradient
propagation and serve as control signals for multimodal interaction.
 
 
\subsection{Role-Matched Reliability-Guided Interaction}
\label{sec:interaction}
 
Reliability is useful only if it regulates information flow in a manner
consistent with the role of the corresponding representation.
SARM therefore uses different interaction mechanisms for shared and
modality-specific information:
shared representations form a reliability-weighted consensus, whereas
specific representations exchange complementary information
directionally.
 
Relative reliability determines which representations should
contribute more strongly.
However, normalized weights alone cannot suppress an interaction when
all participating representations are weak.
For a set of reliability scores $\mathcal{S}$, we therefore define the
joint gate:
%
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_{\mathcal{S}}
    =
    1-
    \prod_{j\in\mathcal{S}}
    (1-r_j).
    \label{eq:joint_gate}
\end{equation}
%
Relative reliability controls the contribution of individual
representations, while the joint gate controls the overall interaction
strength.
 
 
\paragraph{Shared consensus.}
For the shared subspace, the joint gate and normalized reliability
weights are:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \gamma_i^{c}
    &=
    1-
    \prod_{m\in\mathcal{M}}
    (1-r_{m,i}^{c}),\\
    w_{m,i}^{c}
    &=
    \frac{
        r_{m,i}^{c}
    }{
        \sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}}
        r_{n,i}^{c}
    }.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shared_weights}
\end{equation}
 
The shared consensus is then:
%
\begin{equation}
    \overline{S}_i
    =
    \gamma_i^{c}
    \sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}
    w_{m,i}^{c}S_{m,i}.
    \label{eq:shared_consensus}
\end{equation}
%
Representations with stronger shared support contribute more to the
consensus, while the joint gate reduces its strength when the available
shared evidence is collectively weak.
 
 
\paragraph{Specific sender--receiver exchange.}
Modality-specific representations remain separate and exchange
complementary information directionally.
For receiver modality $m$ and sender modality $n$, we obtain:
%
\begin{equation}
    B_{m\leftarrow n,i}
    =
    \operatorname{CrossAttn}
    \left(
        P_{m,i},
        P_{n,i}
    \right),
    \label{eq:specific_message}
\end{equation}
%
where the receiver provides the query and the sender provides the key
and value.
 
Their joint gate and sender influence are:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \gamma_{m,n,i}^{s}
    &=
    1-
    (1-r_{m,i}^{s})
    (1-r_{n,i}^{s}),\\
    \beta_{m\leftarrow n,i}
    &=
    \gamma_{m,n,i}^{s}
    \frac{
        r_{n,i}^{s}
    }{
        r_{m,i}^{s}+r_{n,i}^{s}
    }.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:specific_weights}
\end{equation}
 
The receiver is updated toward the sender-conditioned representation:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \widetilde{P}_{m\leftarrow n,i}
    =
    P_{m,i}
    +
    \beta_{m\leftarrow n,i}
    \left(
        B_{m\leftarrow n,i}
        -
        P_{m,i}
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:specific_update}
\end{equation}
%
The relative reliability term increases the influence of a
better-supported sender, while the joint gate suppresses the exchange
when both representations are weak.
 
For multiple senders, the resulting representations are combined as:
%
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{P}_{m,i}
    =
    \mathcal{A}_m
    \left(
        \left\{
            \widetilde{P}_{m\leftarrow n,i}
        \right\}_{n\neq m}
    \right),
    \label{eq:specific_aggregation}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathcal{A}_m$ denotes the specific-feature aggregation
operator.
 
 
\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:objective}
 
The reliability-regulated shared and modality-specific representations
are passed to the downstream sentiment predictor.
Let $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{base}}$ denote the task and
representation-learning objectives of the shared--specific backbone.
The overall objective is:
%
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    =
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{base}}
    +
    \lambda_{\mathrm{CPA}}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CPA}},
    \label{eq:overall_objective}
\end{equation}
%
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{CPA}}\geq0$ controls the auxiliary
shared-space structuring objective
