\subsection{Subspace-Specific Reliability Estimation}
\label{sec:reliability}


Shared and modality-specific representations serve different information roles and therefore require different sources of reliability evidence. MIRRORS assesses shared representations through cross-modal corroboration and modality-specific representations through local support within the same modality. Both estimates are derived from geometrically calibrated representations.



\paragraph{Residual geometric calibration.}
We calibrate pooled representations before computing reliability scores by removing their components along the training-set mean direction and softly normalizing the remaining residuals. The resulting vectors retain residual direction and relative magnitude as geometric support cues for reliability estimation.



For each modality $m$ and subspace $q\in\{c,s\}$, let $\boldsymbol{\mu}_m^q$ denote the unit-normalized mean direction estimated from training representations. We first project the pooled representation onto the orthogonal complement of this direction:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{d}_{m,i}^{q}=\mathbf{v}_{m,i}^{q}-\left[(\mathbf{v}_{m,i}^{q})^\top\boldsymbol{\mu}_m^q\right]\boldsymbol{\mu}_m^q.
    \label{eq:residual_projection}
\end{equation}

Let $\sigma_m^q>0$ denote a residual scale estimated from the average magnitude of training residuals. The calibrated representation is then defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{m,i}^{q}=\frac{\mathbf{d}_{m,i}^{q}}{\sqrt{\|\mathbf{d}_{m,i}^{q}\|_2^2+(\sigma_m^q)^2}},\qquad q\in\{c,s\}.
    \label{eq:geometric_calibration}
\end{equation}

Unlike unit normalization, this transformation preserves a bounded measure of residual strength, with $\|\mathbf{z}_{m,i}^{q}\|_2<1$. Residuals with small magnitude relative to $\sigma_m^q$ remain attenuated, while the normalized magnitude approaches one for sufficiently large residuals. The calibrated vectors are used only for reliability estimation; the original sequence representations remain the inputs to multimodal interaction.

\paragraph{Shared reliability.}
Since shared representations encode cross-modal commonality, their reliability is evaluated through corroboration from the remaining modalities. For sample $i$, we form the reference direction for modality $m$ by aggregating the calibrated shared vectors of the other modalities. Shared uncertainty is defined as
\begin{equation}
    u_{m,i}^{c}=1-(\mathbf{z}_{m,i}^{c})^\top\operatorname{Norm}\left(\sum_{\substack{n\in\mathcal{M}\\n\neq m}}\mathbf{z}_{n,i}^{c}\right).
    \label{eq:shared_uncertainty}
\end{equation}


A larger $u_{m,i}^{c}$ indicates weaker agreement between the calibrated shared representation and the aggregate direction of the remaining modalities. The score incorporates the query's residual strength but does not directly measure the absolute reliability of the reference modalities.


\paragraph{Modality-specific reliability.}
For modality-specific representations, cross-modal agreement is not a suitable criterion because differences between modalities may encode valid complementary information. We instead evaluate whether a specific representation is supported by nearby training representations from the same modality.

For each modality $m$, we construct a reference bank $\mathcal{B}_m^s=\{(\mathbf{b}_{m,j}^{s},j)\mid j\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}$, where $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ denotes the training index set and $\mathbf{b}_{m,j}^{s}=\operatorname{Norm}(\mathbf{d}_{m,j}^{s})$ is the normalized specific residual direction of training sample $j$. Given a query representation $\mathbf{z}_{m,i}^{s}$, its $K_n$ nearest references are selected according to
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{N}_{K_n}^{m}(i)=\operatorname{TopK}_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}},\,j\neq i}\left[(\mathbf{z}_{m,i}^{s})^\top\mathbf{b}_{m,j}^{s}\right].
    \label{eq:specific_neighbors}
\end{equation}

The exclusion $j\neq i$ prevents self-matching when the query belongs to the training set. We then define specific uncertainty using the average support from the retrieved references:
\begin{equation}
    u_{m,i}^{s}=1-\frac{1}{K_n}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{K_n}^{m}(i)}(\mathbf{z}_{m,i}^{s})^\top\mathbf{b}_{m,j}^{s}.
    \label{eq:specific_uncertainty}
\end{equation}


A larger $u_{m,i}^{s}$ indicates weaker local support for the specific representation within the same modality. We use this support as a reliability proxy, assuming that locally coherent representations provide more stable evidence under partial missingness. The retrieved references are used only for reliability estimation and are not injected into the current representation.


\paragraph{Uncertainty-to-reliability mapping.}
The two uncertainty measures are converted into reliability scores using the same monotonic mapping:
\begin{equation}
    r_{m,i}^{q}=\operatorname{sg}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{u_{m,i}^{q}}{\tau}\right)\right],\qquad q\in\{c,s\},
    \label{eq:reliability_mapping}
\end{equation}
where $\tau>0$ controls the decay rate. The resulting scores $r_{m,i}^{c}$ and $r_{m,i}^{s}$ are detached from gradient propagation and used as control signals for their corresponding multimodal interactions.

\subsection{Role-Matched Reliability-Guided Interaction}
\label{sec:interaction}

Shared representations are combined to capture common information, whereas modality-specific representations are retained separately to exchange complementary evidence. MIRRORS accordingly employs reliability-guided consensus for the shared subspace and directional sender--receiver interaction for the specific subspace.

\paragraph{Joint reliability gating.}
Normalized reliability weights determine the relative contribution of participating representations. However, their sum remains one regardless of the absolute reliability levels. To additionally control interaction magnitude, we define a joint gate for a set of reliability scores $\mathcal{R}$:
\begin{equation}
    \gamma(\mathcal{R})=1-\prod_{r\in\mathcal{R}}(1-r).
    \label{eq:joint_gate}
\end{equation}
The gate satisfies $0\leq\gamma(\mathcal{R})\leq1$ and increases with each participating reliability score. It is used together with relative reliability weights in the two interaction mechanisms.

\paragraph{Reliability-guided shared consensus.}
For the shared subspace, we compute the normalized contribution of each modality and the joint gate as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    w_{m,i}^{c}&=\frac{r_{m,i}^{c}}{\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}}r_{n,i}^{c}},\\
    \gamma_i^{c}&=\gamma(\{r_{m,i}^{c}\}_{m\in\mathcal{M}}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shared_weights}
\end{equation}

The shared representations are then aggregated into a reliability-guided consensus:
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\mathbf{S}}_i=\gamma_i^{c}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}w_{m,i}^{c}\mathbf{S}_{m,i}.
    \label{eq:shared_consensus}
\end{equation}


Here, $w_{m,i}^{c}$ determines the relative contribution of modality $m$, while $\gamma_i^{c}$ scales the aggregated shared representation according to the participating reliability scores.


\paragraph{Reliability-guided directional exchange.}
Unlike shared representations, modality-specific representations are not directly merged into a common consensus. Instead, each modality acts as a receiver of complementary information from the remaining modalities. For receiver $m$ and sender $n$, we obtain a sender-conditioned representation through cross-attention:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{B}_{m\leftarrow n,i}=\operatorname{CrossAttn}(\mathbf{P}_{m,i},\mathbf{P}_{n,i}),
    \label{eq:specific_message}
\end{equation}
where the receiver provides the query and the sender provides the key and value.

To regulate this exchange, we combine the relative reliability of the sender with a pairwise joint gate:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \gamma_{m,n,i}^{s}&=1-(1-r_{m,i}^{s})(1-r_{n,i}^{s}),\\
    \beta_{m\leftarrow n,i}&=\gamma_{m,n,i}^{s}\frac{r_{n,i}^{s}}{r_{m,i}^{s}+r_{n,i}^{s}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:specific_weights}
\end{equation}

The receiver representation is updated toward the sender-conditioned representation according to
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{m\leftarrow n,i}=\mathbf{P}_{m,i}+\beta_{m\leftarrow n,i}\left(\mathbf{B}_{m\leftarrow n,i}-\mathbf{P}_{m,i}\right).
    \label{eq:specific_update}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $\beta_{m\leftarrow n,i}$ increases with sender reliability and decreases with receiver reliability when the other score is fixed. Moreover, $0\leq\beta_{m\leftarrow n,i}\leq r_{n,i}^{s}$, so the sender's estimated reliability bounds its influence on the pairwise update.


When multiple senders are available, their pairwise outputs are combined through the specific-feature aggregation operator:
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{m,i}=\mathcal{A}_m\left(\{\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{m\leftarrow n,i}\}_{n\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\{m\}}\right),
    \label{eq:specific_aggregation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_m$ denotes the aggregation operation used to combine the sender-conditioned representations of receiver modality $m$. Further properties of the reliability estimators and interaction coefficients are provided in the Appendix.
